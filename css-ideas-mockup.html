Idea 1

Scroll reveal works today

Cards lift into place as they enter the viewport. No JavaScript, no scroll listeners, no library — the browser drives the animation off scroll position itself.

The tuning that matters: the distance and the range. Below is deliberately gentle — 22px of travel, finished by the time a card is a quarter of the way up. Anything more and the page starts to feel like it's loading rather than moving. It's also fully off for anyone with reduced-motion turned on.

Idea 3

Anchor-positioned tooltips newer browsers

Hover or tap lagomlagom — Swedish. Not too little, not too much. The right amount, and that being the point rather than a compromise. and the definition pins itself to the word. Try it near the edge too: hyggehygge — Danish. The warmth of a small, well-lit, unhurried moment. Usually involves being indoors while it is miserable outside.

Why this is different from what you'd have built before: keeping a tooltip on screen near an edge has always meant JavaScript measuring the viewport and flipping the box. Here the browser does it — you list fallback positions and it picks the first that fits. For Geek Word posts that means a definition attached to the term itself instead of a footnote the reader has to go find.

Your browser doesn't support this yet, so you're seeing the fallback: the definition sits inline underneath. That's the honest degradation — nothing breaks, it just stops floating.

Idea 4

Colours derived from your tokens newer browsers

Both bands look similar. The difference is where the colours come from.

Hand-coded today

Derived from --color-terra

Every swatch below is the same one terracotta token at a different lightness and saturation — none of these hex values exist anywhere in the stylesheet.

Nothing looks different the day you ship it. The payoff is the next time you change a brand colour: today every sky gradient stop is a hand-picked hex, so changing terracotta means re-tuning all of them by hand. Written this way they're expressed as oklch(from var(--color-terra) …) — "the same hue, lighter" — so they move with the token automatically. It's a maintenance change, not a visual one, which is exactly why it's easy to keep putting off.
